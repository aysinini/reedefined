"I deleted my algorithm and dressed only for myself for thirty days."

A month without recommendations, without trends, without the quiet pressure of a feed. Just a wardrobe, a mirror, and thirty mornings.
A
Ayse Yilmaz
May 2026 · Issue No. 5
8 min read

It started the way most experiments do — with a small irritation that became impossible to ignore. I had spent forty minutes scrolling through a feed of outfits I admired but would never wear, trying to decide what to put on for a Tuesday morning. At some point I looked up from my phone and realised I was standing in front of a wardrobe full of clothes I had chosen, at various points in my life, because they made me feel something.

"I had been outsourcing my taste to an algorithm that had never once met me."

So I made a rule. For thirty days, no fashion content. No TikTok outfit videos, no Instagram saved folders, no Pinterest boards. I would dress only from what I already owned, guided only by what I actually felt like wearing when I opened the wardrobe in the morning.

By day three, something unexpected happened. I stopped feeling anxious about getting dressed. Without the constant backdrop of other people's choices, I started noticing what I actually reached for — and what had been hanging untouched for two years despite being, objectively, a perfectly good coat.

The algorithm had not been showing me myself. It had been showing me a version of me it was trying to create: someone who bought more, clicked more, stayed longer. My actual taste — quieter, more specific, harder to monetise — had never been particularly useful to it.
